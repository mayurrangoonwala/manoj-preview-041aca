Mortgage refinancing

Refinancing replaces your mortgage with a new one, usually to lower your payments, pay off high-interest debt, or take equity out of your home.

Start the 2-minute surveyCall 647-824-8251

Common reasons to refinance

  • Rolling credit cards and loans into one lower monthly payment
  • Taking equity out for renovations or a family need
  • Buying out a former partner after a separation
  • Moving to a better rate once your situation has improved

How much you can borrow

You can usually refinance up to about 80% of your home's value, minus what you still owe on it.

When your bank says no

If your income or credit has changed since you got your mortgage, your bank may not approve a refinance. A B-lender, or a short-term private second mortgage, can bridge the gap.

The cost of breaking early

Refinancing before your term ends usually means a prepayment penalty. I work out whether the savings beat the penalty before you decide anything.

Questions about refinancing

Expect a legal fee, usually an appraisal, and a penalty if you're mid-term. I lay out the full cost next to the savings so you can see whether it's worth it.

Mortgage renewal

When your term ends, your lender sends a renewal offer, and many people simply sign it. It's the one time you can move without a penalty, so it's worth a second look.

Start the 2-minute surveyCall 647-824-8251

Why shop your renewal

Renewal offers are not always the best rate the lender has. Comparing before you sign can save real money over the next term, and switching at maturity has no prepayment penalty.

When to start

Start about four months before your term ends. Many lenders will hold a rate for up to 120 days, so you can lock in early and still take a lower rate if one appears.

If things have changed

If your income has dropped, you are now self-employed, or your credit has taken a hit, a straight renewal may be all your bank offers. I can check whether a better option exists, including with B-lenders.

My bank won't renew me. What now?

It happens more than people think: missed payments during a hard year, a drop in income, or a lender that's leaving your type of mortgage. Sometimes the bank will renew but only at a rate that's hard to accept.

You still have options. Your current lender may renew once you're caught up. A B-lender can take over the mortgage when a bank won't. And when credit needs time to recover, a short private mortgage can bridge the gap. The key is to start early, not in the last week.

Renewing with bruised credit

  • Get up to date on any missed payments if you can. It changes what lenders will offer
  • Don't sign the first offer without comparing, even if it feels like your only option
  • Be upfront about what happened. A clear explanation helps more than silence
  • Expect a higher rate for a term or two, and plan to review it at the next renewal

A renewal timeline that works

  • About 4 months out: we compare your lender's position with other lenders, and hold a rate if it makes sense
  • About 3 months out: your lender's offer usually arrives. Send it to me and I'll tell you if it's fair
  • About 2 months out: decide, and start paperwork if you're switching
  • The last month: sign, and your new term starts on your renewal date with no gap

Example scenario

A renewal after a hard year

A homeowner fell behind on two payments after a job change, then caught up. Her bank's renewal offer came in well above the rates she was seeing elsewhere, and two other banks declined to take the mortgage over.

A B-lender accepted the switch, because her payments were current again and she had good equity. The rate was higher than a bank's, but lower than her renewal offer. We review again at her next renewal.

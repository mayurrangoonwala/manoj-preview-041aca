Private mortgages and private lending

When even a B-lender can't help in time, a private mortgage can. It's short-term money secured by your property's equity, used to fix a problem now and set up something better next.

Start the 2-minute surveyCall 647-824-8251

How private lending works

Private mortgages come from individual investors or mortgage investment corporations. Approval rests mainly on the equity in your property rather than your income or credit score, which is why they can often move faster than a bank.

When it makes sense

  • Your closing date is close and your financing fell through
  • You need to consolidate debt but your credit rules out a B-lender for now
  • You want a second mortgage to reach your equity without breaking your first mortgage
  • Your income is hard to document and you need a short bridge while you sort it out

What it costs

Private money is the most expensive option. Rates are higher than B-lenders, terms are usually one year, and there are lender and brokerage fees. That is why I only arrange it with an exit plan agreed up front, usually a longer-term mortgage once the problem is fixed.

Who private lenders are

Private mortgages come from individual investors and mortgage investment corporations. When I arrange one, it's done the proper way: a written commitment, registration on your title, and independent lawyers on both sides.

Typical terms

  • A one-year term, sometimes two
  • Often interest-only payments, which keeps the monthly cost down
  • Lending based mainly on equity, usually up to around 75–80% of the home's value combined
  • A lender fee and a brokerage fee, both disclosed in writing before you commit

Red flags to avoid

  • Anyone asking for a large fee upfront, before you have a written approval
  • Pressure to sign quickly without your own lawyer reviewing it
  • Terms that aren't in writing, or that change at the last minute
  • No plan for how you'll get out of the mortgage when the term ends

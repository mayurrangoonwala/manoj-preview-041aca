B-lender mortgages in Ontario

A bank decline isn't the end of the road. B-lenders are regulated trust companies and credit unions that look at the files the big banks turn away, and they're where most of my clients start.

What a B-lender is

B-lenders are regulated lenders, such as trust companies and credit unions, that apply more flexible rules than the big banks. They look at things a bank's system rejects automatically: income that doesn't come on a T4, a short credit history, or a recent rough patch.

Who it suits

  • Self-employed people whose tax returns don't show what the business really earns
  • Newcomers with less than two years of Canadian credit history
  • Anyone with recent credit trouble: missed payments, collections, or a past decline
  • Borrowers the bank turned down on debt ratios alone

What it costs

Rates are typically 1–3% higher than a bank's, and most B-lenders charge a lender fee, often around 1% of the mortgage. Some files also carry a brokerage fee. I tell you every number before you commit, so nothing is new on closing day.

At renewal

B-lender terms are usually one or two years. Before each renewal I review your file and your options, and tell you plainly which is best.

Signs a B-lender is your best route

  • Your income is real, but your tax return shows less after business write-offs
  • You've been in your job, or in Canada, for less than two years
  • You had missed payments, collections or a consumer proposal, and you're rebuilding
  • Your debt ratios are a little over the bank's limits
  • The bank simply said no, and nobody explained why

How I work a B-lender file

  • A first call, by phone or video, to hear the whole story, including the parts that worry you
  • You upload documents securely online; nothing has to be dropped off
  • I write the lender a cover note that explains your file in plain terms, because a file that's explained gets read differently
  • Approval, conditions and the appraisal: I chase each one and keep you posted
  • Your lawyer closes, and documents can usually be signed electronically
  • Before your renewal, I review your options with you

What you'll usually need

  • Photo ID
  • Proof of income: pay stubs and a job letter, or Notices of Assessment and bank statements if you're self-employed
  • Recent bank statements showing your down payment or equity
  • A short explanation of any credit problems, in your own words. I'll help you write it

Example scenario

A self-employed buyer

A contractor in Brampton has run his business for four years. After write-offs, his tax return shows about half of what the business really deposits, so his bank declines the purchase.

A B-lender looks at twelve months of business bank statements instead. With 20% down, the file fits their self-employed program. The rate is higher than the bank's, and the term is one year, so before it ends we review his options together.
